By this point, we know what the "dead internet theory" is. It's where bots and computers make up the majority of internet usage, overtaking us humans. Botfarms have existed for a long time. The recent, rapid advancements in AI has amplifled this theory towards reality. Let's enslave LLMs to mass produce comments and posts. Steering a narrative has never been easier. The dead internet reality is here.

## Let's not move too far. Why can bots even take over?

The ease of automating web requests. Bots are run on computer programs instructed by a human. I mean, the whole time your Instagram, X, YouTube, or any apps that use the internet are always some type of computer program to make those requests. *Shocking,* isn't it? Bots run a program that's an ultra minimalistic version of a web browser, it can only make requests to sites/apps it was instructed to. Other than that, it can also be instructed to make massive amounts of requests in a short amount of time.

You can imagine lots of examples of the usage of bots in social media, some of which would be spamming Instagram comments with multiple accounts, automating scam/spam posts on X, etc.

## What role does AI have here?

A big one. It can do such thing said previously with less human input. You can definitely hook it up to social platforms and generate narratives in realtime with only a couple presses. As of writing, most LLMs can use tools in order to do so.

Compared to bots, LLMs can generate text that fit a certain narrative on the fly. It can definitely fit in with the humans who are discussing about that narrative, hence it can muddy the discourse with ease.

With it getting increasingly better in capabilities, it can only get scarier from here.

## Why should we worry about it?

With the passing of time, it is getting harder and harder to distinguish between what is real and what isn't. Ever since Anthropic released their [*Claude Opus 5.5*](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-opus-5-5) model, my feed has been bombarded with LLM-generated reels.

I was peeling soybean skins the skin off of the soybeans I bought that I'd then use to make soy milk. It's a very important step to make the milk not smell weird. Thing is, the process is very tedious and you have to be careful to make sure the skin has peeled off. That assurance After I had gone through that all of process, I felt like I had accomplished something and I had gotten that assurance of the beans no longer having the skin. 

But, _**I'm not solely talking about soybeans.**_

Let's take this to my favorite hobby, programming, or coding, or whatever you might call it. Writing code by hand is like peeling soybeans, you also read it and make sure everything goes well. One bug is like one unpeeled soybean you have to, well, peel. You peel it, and boom, problem solved!

Now, what if we let another person peel the soybeans instead of ourselves? We're then not sure if the soybeans are fully peeled. This is the importance of reviewing code from someone else. If there happens to be an unpeeled bean, we simply tell them to peel it. But what if we didn't do that? It's obvious, isn't it?

## My personal experience

Personally, I was vibecoding before ChatGPT even existed. Me and a friend were making a silly project using GitHub Copilot. At the time, all it did was autocomplete, but to us, it was revolutionary that we can prompt it and it can generate (slightly) working code for us. When ChatGPT was first released, I immediately hopped on and started to code using it. At first, it was actually very useful, I had learnt how HTTP requests work, how `useState` works in React, and some cute frontend tidbits.

Let's move forward... a year to 2024. There's a hot code editor on the rise called [Cursor](https://cursor.com), where it enables LLMs to observe a codebase and make small modifications to it upon human request. This was the start of a new era of AI-assisted coding. By that point, AI can generate actual working code, and it can also interact with the LSP (language server protocol) for error checking and it can modify... only upon human request at the time. But this is the precursor to what we all love and always delegate to, semi-autonomous AI agents.

In early 2025, Andrej Karpathy, one of the known researchers in AI who previously worked for OpenAI, Tesla, and currently Anthropic, coined the term "vibecoding". It's a new style of AI-assisted coding where you follow the vibes and simply forget about the code, just feel the vibes. After this, the advancements of LLMs get even faster and faster as the time moves, and by this point it can literally make a project on its own.

As implied in the thesis, it's quite detrimental and it could mean everything could fall apart. Here's my main criticism: If we offload too much of our thinking to AI, we easily lose control of our work. We don't know what soybeans are already peeled and what aren't yet peeled, and the end result could end up stinky. You don't get that "eureka!" feeling when you vibecode. As a heavy vibecoder myself the effects are detrimental. I feel like I've developed cognitive debt, exactly lots of it. Everytime I get stuck, I often resort to AI. That problem solving skill slowly fades away, hence, the ability to think too. It gets to a point where our brains... "die"? ChatGPT this, Claude that, when will it finish?? It's like a smoking addiction. You won't stop until you force yourself to.

## How do I resurrect my brain?

I resort to write essays like these, and try to start coding without AI little by little. It may suck at first, but you'll get used to no longer resort everything to AI and have control over your work again! But, it's easier said than done. You have a new frontier LLM every week or two nowadays and it'll be demotivating that your favorite hobby just gets taken away by AI like that. Keep doing what keeps you _feeling alive._ And again most importantly, _**stay critical of the moment.**_
